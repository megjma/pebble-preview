Freestyle · Channel Strategy

What is DTC actually for?

Decide DTC's job before optimizing it. For a diaper brand, DTC is not the volume engine and not the margin engine — it's the data, relationship, brand, and innovation engine, and the home of your highest-LTV subscribers. Today that engine runs on ~3% of revenue — far below where it earns its keep.

What DTC is
  • A data asset — the only channel where you learn who the customer is, their baby's stage, and true LTV.
  • A retention relationship — the home of your highest-value, longest-tenure subscribers.
  • The brand's full voice — the premium-at-a-fair-price and plastic-negative story a shelf tag can't tell.
  • A test lab — where new sizes, bundles, and pricing prove out before retail.
What DTC is not
  • Not the volume engine — category volume lives on the retail shelf.
  • Not a margin play — shipping a heavy product offsets the retailer margin it would save.
  • Not a cheaper Amazon — Amazon owns price and convenience; a discount is not a moat.
  • Not for every customer — occasional and price-driven buyers are better served, at better margin, by retail.

01 · The goal

Growing DTC from ~3%

At ~3% of revenue, DTC is too small to do its job — you can't run a data, relationship, or innovation engine on 3%. The goal isn't a target % — it's escaping sub-scale so the flywheel starts to turn.
  • 3% is the real cost, not just a revenue line — it means almost no first-party data, almost no direct tie to your best customers, and no test lab for sizes, bundles, or pricing.
  • Grow it through the two levers that follow — a genuine reason to subscribe (②) and a retail→DTC conversion loop (③) — not by discounting into a channel Amazon already owns.
  • Be clear-eyed: owned DTC is hard in diapers — even the pioneer retreated (see right). The winners are the differentiated few, like Coterie. DTC growth here must be earned with a real reason to exist (②); it won't come from auto-ship alone.

02 · The reason to exist

Why subscribe here, not Target or Amazon?

Today the pull is mostly price — which Amazon can always match. The priority is a structural, only-here reason to choose DTC:
  • Automatic size-up — the spine. The product a baby needs changes every few weeks; wrong size = leaks = churn. Only a data-owning direct relationship can proactively size you up. "We grow with your baby."
  • Price-lock + legible value. Your wedge is premium-at-a-fair-price; DTC locks the lowest price ($59.50 vs Coterie $95 / Parasol $84). Amazon floats; Target won't discount.
  • The whole-routine bundle — diapers + wipes + cream, sized and timed as one box the shelf can't assemble.
  • First access to new sizes, prints, SKUs — a reason for loyalists to stay direct.
  • Eco-impact identity ("plastic kept out of landfills") — retention glue on your net-plastic-negative story, not the acquisition reason.

03 · The loop

Discovery engine, or substitute?

Today it's accidental, not intentional — and traffic leaks toward retail. The design goal is a deliberate discovery→DTC loop: retail catches everyone; DTC converts the loyalists.
  • Retail = trial engine: great for acquiring the first try, wrong tool for owning the relationship.
  • Design the hand-off: on-pack QR / in-box insert → DTC first order + size quiz; retarget retail-driven visitors; a trial pack as the bridge to subscription.
  • Keep paid traffic in-house: owned ads and the PDP shouldn't route hard-won visitors to Amazon/Target unless it's a deliberate margin call.
  • It's fine that some customers end at retail — occasional and price-driven buyers. Force no one to DTC; measure and optimize retail→DTC conversion instead (which needs the data from ①).
Margin reality · shipping economics

Shipping a heavy product reshapes the DTC margin math

Diapers are bulky, heavy, low value-density, and replenished constantly — and dimensional weight, not actual weight, sets the freight cost. On a ~$70 order, shipping can absorb ~10–20% of revenue before pick/pack and processing, so the 30–40 points saved by cutting out the retailer are substantially — sometimes fully — offset. On unit contribution, retail can be the stronger channel. DTC's advantage is therefore the freight-proof value it creates — data, LTV, brand, innovation — and DTC margin is protected by raising revenue per shipment. The clearest proof: The Honest Company — the DTC-subscription diaper pioneer — shut down its owned e-commerce in December 2025 on margin grounds, shifting to Walmart, Target and Amazon. Grow DTC differentiated and margin-disciplined, or repeat that outcome.

The one-line version

Retail is the volume, trial, and often better-unit-margin channel for a product we'd otherwise pay to ship. DTC is the data, relationship, brand, and innovation engine — its edge is not margin per unit. We win DTC by being the only place that grows with your baby, locks your price, and delivers the whole routine — then run retail as a deliberate, instrumented trial funnel that feeds it.
