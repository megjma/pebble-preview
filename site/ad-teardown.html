Diaper Wars: The Meta Ad-Library Deep Dive

Freestyle vs. Coterie, Millie Moon, Parasol & Peachies — every active ad, funnel, and creative angle, mapped for growth.

Executive summary

Freestyle competes in premium DTC diapers against advertisers running 4–7× its ad volume. The upside: it is the cheapest premium diaper in the set and is scaling creative faster than anyone. The job now is to convert that price advantage into acquisition — through more volume, a sharper funnel, and stronger proof.

Where we stand

Out-gunned on volume, moving fast

~26 active ads vs. Millie Moon ~130 and Coterie ~120 — the smallest library in the set, but up +37% in eight days. Volume is the input the category leaders compound on.

The opening

Freestyle owns the value lane

At $70/mo ($59.50 with code GET15), Freestyle is the cheapest premium diaper here — a position none of the three premium DTC rivals will follow. Parasol is the closest twin ("RashShield" vs. "Skin♥Shield") and sits $14 higher.

The biggest lever

Funnel & proof, not the ad

Rivals convert with advertorials, cheap trials, and press/award proof. Freestyle routes most clicks to a single product page and carries the fewest reviews in the set (1,450 vs. Coterie's 9,600). Closing that gap is the highest-ROI work.

Priority moves — next 30 days

  1. Keep ramping creative volume toward the category benchmark (top-quartile accounts launch ~189 new ads/week).
  2. Turn the new offer lander into a true advertorial, and add a low-friction trial pack to reduce entry risk.
  3. Make price-comparison creative a permanent pillar — own the value lane before Parasol or a retailer takes it.

02

Two games are being played

The five brands split into two fundamentally different go-to-market models. Freestyle straddles them — DTC subscription with a toe in retail (Walmart) — which is an opportunity, not a problem, if pursued deliberately.

Model A · DTC subscription

Own the customer, sell the funnel

Ads drive to owned pages (PDPs or advertorials), convert to a recurring subscription, and compete on funnel craft, offers, and LTV. Social proof and creative volume are the battleground.

FreestyleCoterieParasolPeachies
Model B · Retail omnichannel

Drive the shelf, win on distribution

Ads drive to retailer pages — Target, Walmart, Kroger, Publix, HEB — building brand demand that converts in-store. Competes on price-per-pack, availability, and licensed IP (Ms. Rachel), not subscription funnels.

04

What's working — creative level

The single asset each competitor leans on hardest, by longevity and duplication, plus the plays beneath it.

Millie Moon

Retail demand machine
Workhorse format · ~20 launched in one day (Aug 1)6-second "Diapers so soft they feel like a cloud ☁️ — at Target from $8.99."
  • Short, cheap, high-volume video — mostly 6-second product spots, produced at scale (~130 live).
  • Licensed IP: a Ms. Rachel collaboration on training pants — borrowed audience + instant trust.
  • Softness + value + availability — "CloudTouch™," "97% of parents say softer," always tied to a retailer and a low price.
  • Drives the shelf, not a funnel — ads point to Target/Walmart/grocery, building demand that converts in-store.

Coterie

Proven-winner machine
Workhorse · live 3+ months"Glamour called us the best diapers on the market" — 10+ hrs, no leak.
  • Press & award social proof as the durable, cheap-to-make backbone static.
  • Volume + catalog: ~120 ads across diapers, wipes, 360° pants, skincare, kits.
  • >50% of clicks go to non-diaper pages — catalog monetization, not just the hero SKU.
  • Never discounts — entry is the Newborn Starter Kit, not a coupon.

Peachies

Trial-led DR operator
Workhorse · running since May (3+ months)"Thinking of switching nappies? Here's why thousands switch… try for £12."
  • Advertorial funnel: most clicks hit "/lp/upgrade-your-nappy-game" (with a bottom-of-funnel variant), not a raw PDP.
  • £12 trial pack is the entire hook — low-risk entry, then subscription.
  • Award + stat proof: "UK's Best Nappy 2025," "98% recommend," "92% fewer leaks."
  • Switch/comparison framing — positions against the incumbent the parent already uses.

Parasol

Freestyle's near-twin · diapers & pants
Diaper workhorse · scaled Aug 11, 4+ versions"Meet the softest diaper built for real-life absorbency — a dual-core system that holds up to 4 cups of liquid."
  • Positioning twin: "RashShield® skin-first" vs. Freestyle's Skin♥Shield — same chlorine-free, Dermatest "Excellent," "zero nasties" story, near-identical name.
  • Diaper ads are ~93% static — long benefit-bullet copy, almost no video.
  • Expanding the baby line: RashShield Diaper Pants for crawlers/toddlers (their most-duplicated recent push) + disposable bibs.
  • 20% off first order is the standing diaper hook.
  • Watch: a physician-designed postpartum line runs alongside — plausibly a pre-birth acquisition wedge that feeds future diaper customers.

The through-line for Freestyle: Peachies routes cold traffic through advertorials before the PDP; Coterie spreads clicks across a catalog; everyone offers a cheap trial ($8.99 / £12 / 20% off). Freestyle just launched a dedicated offer lander (go.freestyle.world, ~35% of ad clicks now) — the right instinct, but it's an Unbounce promo page whose buttons bounce visitors straight to the main PDP to convert: a pre-sell bridge, not a standalone funnel or an editorial advertorial. The site also still carries the fewest reviews in the set. Turning that bridge into a true advertorial + growing proof is the biggest remaining conversion lever.
07

Where Freestyle grows next

Prioritized for ecommerce growth — each maps to a specific gap this five-brand set exposed.

1
Do now

Keep ramping creative volume — good momentum, still ~5× behind

Freestyle just grew ~19 → ~26 ads in 8 days — real acceleration. But Millie Moon (~130) and Coterie (~120) still test at ~5× the scale, and Northbeam's top quartile launched ~189 ads/week. Keep the production line running (brief → static + short video + UGC variants) and hold the weekly cadence; volume is the input that makes every rec below pay off.

2
Do now

Extend the new lander into a real advertorial + add a trial pack

Freestyle already stood up go.freestyle.world — a dedicated 15%-off offer lander now taking ~35% of clicks. Right instinct; next step is to make it a true editorial advertorial (hook → "why parents switch" story → comparison table vs. Coterie/Parasol → testimonials), like Peachies' "/upgrade-your-nappy-game." And add a low-friction trial pack — every competitor has a cheap entry ($8.99 retail, £12, 20% off); a trial beats a 50%-off subscription commitment on perceived risk.

3
Do now

Out-position Parasol & win the value lane outright

Parasol is your near-clone — "RashShield skin-first" vs. your "Skin♥Shield" — but sits at $84 to your $70 ($59.50 with GET15), and Coterie at $95. And Parasol is scaling its RashShield diaper + pants line (total ~44 → ~60 ads), so the window matters. Make explicit price-comparison creative a permanent pillar (same EWG/TCF/absorbency credentials, meaningfully cheaper). You own the value lane; defend it before Parasol or a retail brand takes it.

4
Do next

Close the social-proof gap & add award/press proof

At 1,450 reviews you have the least proof on the most-trafficked page, while Coterie (press quote) and Peachies ("UK's Best Nappy," 98% recommend) run proof as their signature format. Aggressively grow reviews and land one award/press mention to build the same cheap, months-long-running static.

5
Do next

Expand the catalog — diapers-only is leaving money on the table

Coterie monetizes skincare + kits, Millie Moon added Ms. Rachel training pants, Parasol added diaper pants + bibs. Freestyle's training pants have no DTC PDP (Walmart only) and wipes only just got one. Build dedicated PDPs + bundles and a low-friction trial/starter kit to lift AOV and widen the funnel — and, as an acquisition play, weigh a pre-birth SKU (the wedge Parasol runs) to capture parents before the diaper decision is made.

6
Do next

Decide the retail question deliberately

Millie Moon proves a retail-first ad model (drive to Target/Walmart) can out-scale DTC on volume. Freestyle already sells training pants on Walmart. Either lean in — run retail-driving ads and pursue shelf placement — or consciously stay DTC-pure. Right now it's half-in by accident; make it a strategy.
