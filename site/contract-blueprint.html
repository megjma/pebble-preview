The Contract Blueprint

What the Freestyle creator agreement needs to cover, and what compensation should look like — guidelines for the team setting up contracts in Growi, and the spec to hand your legal team.

This is not a contract. It's the requirements list. Legal drafts (or approves) the actual language before the first creator signs; Growi then handles e-signature, payment structure, and records.

Part one

What the contract must cover

Ten things, each with the reason it's there — so nothing gets negotiated away without knowing what it protects.

01No posting quota — the hybrid model

State plainly that there's no minimum posting requirement; creators post if and when they want. Staying on the active roster for future product drops requires at least one published post showing real product use — and an unboxing alone doesn't qualify.

Why: keeps the gift-energy program creators actually join, while the "post to stay" mechanic protects volume. Critical drafting rule: the qualifying post must be defined by use, never sentiment — requiring a post is legal; requiring a favorable post violates the FTC Reviews Rule.

02Compensation never tied to sentiment

An explicit line that no compensation, perk, or roster status depends on what the creator says or how positively they say it.

Why: this sentence is the Reviews Rule shield (~$50k+ per violation) and it also documents the own-voice program design.

03FTC disclosure obligation

Disclosure on every post: plain language (#ad / "Freestyle sent me these" / "I earn a commission from my link") at the top of the caption, spoken in the first seconds of video plus on-screen text, and the platform's paid-partnership tool on top. Free product or commission eligibility alone triggers it.

Why: the brand is liable for creator non-disclosure; the contract makes the duty explicit and enforceable.

04Content standards + the 3 Rules as a signed exhibit

The danger-words list in contract form (no prevents/treats/cures, hypoallergenic, chemical-free, non-toxic, biodegradable/eco terms, flushable, safest, guaranteed, expert endorsements, statistics, or named-competitor comparisons) — and the "3 Rules" creator brief attached as an exhibit the signature acknowledges.

Why: the e-signature then doubles as the written guideline acknowledgment the FTC expects from a real compliance program — zero extra process.

05Correction window

If we flag a post as non-compliant (disclosure or danger words), the creator fixes or removes it within 48 hours; repeat issues end the partnership.

Why: the enforcement mechanism regulators look for, agreed to upfront instead of argued about later.

06Retail review ban

No ratings or reviews on retail platforms (Amazon, Target.com, etc.) in connection with the partnership, unless the platform permits incentivized reviews and the review discloses the free product.

Why: incentivized retail reviews are the Reviews Rule's core target.

07Usage rights — the whole point of the program

A non-exclusive license for Freestyle to repost deliverables on its own organic channels (with credit) for a set window — 12 months is a fair default. Pre-negotiate a flat paid-usage rate in the same contract (see compensation) so licensing a winner into ads is one click, not a negotiation. Creator keeps ownership; we can't edit content in ways that change its meaning.

Why: without signed usage rights, every great video requires chasing the creator for permission before it can become an ad — the ad-creative harvest depends on this clause.

08Independent contractor, non-exclusive

Creators are contractors, not employees or agents; the deal is non-exclusive (with an optional short category-exclusivity window around each post if we ever want it).

Why: avoids employment-law exposure and keeps the offer light enough for nanos to sign.

09Term, termination, and survival

Either side can walk away on written notice (a Growi message counts). Earned commissions stay payable; disclosure duties and usage rights survive for content already published.

Why: "survival" is the piece teams forget — without it, a creator who quits could demand their content pulled from your ads.

10Original work & consent

Content is the creator's original work, doesn't infringe third-party rights (music licensing per platform rules matters most), and everyone appearing has consented — with the creator consenting on behalf of their own minor children.

Why: the babies are the content; publicity/consent language for minors is a must-ask for legal (see below).

Part two

What compensation should look like

The gift
One product drop (~$40 diapers + wipes) per cohort, no strings.Re-up drops go to creators who posted at least one in-use piece — the roster mechanic, not an obligation.
Commission
15% of attributed net sales to start (room to raise to 20% for proven performers), tracked link/code, paid monthly through Growi.Commission is the engine that makes voluntary posting happen — it needs no quota to work.
Flat fees
None at the 2–10k tier.Product + commission is the market-standard offer here; fees enter later for retainer-level ambassadors only.
Paid usage
Pre-negotiated flat rate of $150–250 per asset licensed into ads (whitelisting/Spark Ads), baked into every contract.Turns "can we run this as an ad?" into a payment, not a negotiation — and at this tier it's a thrilling bonus for the creator.
Rotating bonuses
One or two active bonuses each month/quarter, announced in Growi and rotated to keep the program fresh — the volume-and-quality engine (menu below).Every bonus keys off behavior or metrics, never sentiment.

★The rotating bonus menu

Pick 1–2 per period and publish the terms in Growi before the period starts. Every earned bonus gets paid, always — there's no "pool cap" that can void an earned offer. Cost is controlled by structure instead: use fixed-payout formats (contests, leaderboards, creator of the month) for the bigger dollars, size open offers so the worst case is affordable (e.g., $50 × 100 creators = $5,000 absolute ceiling — budget on worst case × realistic hit rate), and tune next month's offer if one over-performs. Never change terms mid-period. Numbers below are starting points:

  • Volume bonus: $50–75 for publishing 3+ qualifying videos (in-use, non-unboxing) in the month.
  • Engagement bonus: $100 to the top 10 qualifying posts by engagement rate for follower size (a leaderboard — fixed payout by design, and the fairer bar for nanos than a flat views number).
  • Conversion bonus: commission bumps to 20% (or a flat $) past a GMV milestone — Growi automates these thresholds natively.
  • Product perks: an extra or upsized product drop for a 2–3 month posting streak; first access to new products for the most consistent posters.
  • Creator of the month: $150–250 + a feature on Freestyle's channels, selected on objective performance (views, saves, attributed sales) — never on how flattering the content was.

Why rotate: a fresh bonus each period re-engages the quiet middle of the roster (the enthusiasts post anyway); publishing terms upfront in Growi keeps every bonus a documented, sentiment-neutral offer. Two compliance notes for whoever writes each month's bonus: (1) qualify posts by format and use (video, product in use), never by tone — "3 great posts about how much you love Freestyle" is an illegal incentive, "3 videos showing Freestyle in use" is a clean one; (2) bonuses are compensation, so the disclosure duty on every post is unchanged.

The pre-publish ritual: before any bonus goes live, do the 30-second worst-case math — "$50 for 3 videos" × 100-creator roster = $5,000 absolute ceiling, realistically $1,000–1,500 at a 20–30% hit rate. If the ceiling would hurt, restructure as a top-N contest before publishing; never shrink a promise after the fact. And keep the safety valve in mind: if a volume bonus blows past forecast, we got flooded with in-use videos at ~$17–25 apiece — the cheapest content acquisition in the entire program, not a budget failure.

Compensation must never
  • Be conditioned on positive sentiment, a good review, or "loving it" — in the contract, in Growi settings, or in any DM to a creator
  • Pay for retail-site reviews
  • Penalize a creator for honest, less-than-glowing content (not posting is fine; punishing candor is not)

Part three

Setup mechanics in Growi

  • Build one contract template in Growi's contract flow with the legal-approved terms; per-campaign fields (commission %, usage window, product list) live in the campaign/brief.
  • Attach the 3 Rules brief as the exhibit so every e-signature is a recorded guideline acknowledgment.
  • Keep the campaign brief free of product statistics — briefs describe experiences to explore (overnight dryness, routines), never claims, so there's nothing to paraphrase.
  • Export/retain the signed-contract records — that archive is the compliance paper trail.

Part four

Questions for the legal team

Hand this whole page over, and specifically ask them to nail down:

  1. Minors on camera — the right consent/publicity language for creators' children appearing in content we later run as ads, state by state.
  2. License scope — exact wording for the organic repost license vs. the paid-usage license (placements, edit rights, duration, revocability).
  3. Governing law and venue — plus whether we accept international creators (tax forms and FTC-equivalent rules abroad).
  4. The post-to-stay mechanic — confirm our sentiment-neutral wording sits safely inside the FTC Reviews Rule.
  5. Termination and survival — that usage rights and disclosure duties survive for published content.
