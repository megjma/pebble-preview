# Freestyle Responsibility Map — Who Owns What

**Purpose:** internal discussion doc for dividing advertising-compliance responsibilities between Freestyle and our creators. Talk-through version for the partnerships team.

---

## The one-sentence legal reality

**The FTC holds the brand responsible for creator content — even things a creator says that we never told them to say.** A creator posting "cured my baby's eczema" is legally *Freestyle's* unsubstantiated health claim. That's why the split below puts almost all the structural work on us, and only three simple duties on them.

---

## What Freestyle owns (us)

| Task | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| **Substantiation** | Every factual claim we or creators use ("55% more absorbent than the #1 brand," "free of phthalates") must have current testing on file *before* it appears anywhere. Retest when our product or a competitor's product changes. |
| **The approved claims list** | Maintain one canonical list of exact, quotable claim language (with required qualifiers). Creators quote it verbatim or don't use it. |
| **Onboarding & acknowledgment** | Every creator gets the guidelines and confirms in writing they've read them — before the first post. Keep the records. |
| **Contracts** | Partnership agreements include an FTC-compliance clause and the approved-claims annex. |
| **Monitoring** | Actually check tagged posts (monthly spot-checks minimum). The FTC treats unenforced guidelines as no guidelines. |
| **Correction & documentation** | When a post breaks the rules: ask for the fix, confirm it happened (target: 48 hours), and document it. Repeat offenders get paused or terminated — also documented. |
| **Reviews Rule hygiene** | Never pay for, incentivize, or condition anything on a positive review; disclosure baked into any product-seeding program; no suppressing negative reviews. Penalties run ~$50k+ per violation. |
| **Employee & family policy** | Employees and their immediate family must disclose the employment relationship on any Freestyle post, and never post retail-site reviews. |
| **Our own ad copy** | Green Guides (eco claims), FDA line (rash language), NAD-proofing comparative claims — all reviewed before campaigns ship. |

## What creators own (them)

Only three things — everything else is on us:

1. **Disclose the relationship on every post** — clearly, at the top, spoken aloud in videos. Free product counts, not just payment.
2. **Be honest** — actually use the product; only share real opinions and real experiences.
3. **Stay inside the lines** — approved claims quoted exactly; everything else framed as their own experience; never use the danger words (prevents/treats/cures, hypoallergenic, chemical-free, biodegradable, safest, guaranteed, competitor comparisons, made-up numbers).

If a creator does those three things, their personal risk is close to zero — the FTC pursues brands, not individual influencers, except in egregious repeat cases. Tell them this; it lowers the temperature.

## What no one needs to worry about

- **The FCC** — broadcast radio/TV only. Social, podcasts, streaming = FTC territory. Irrelevant unless we buy radio/TV spots.
- **Creators learning the law** — they don't need to understand the Green Guides, the FDA monograph, or NAD precedent. That's why the danger-words list exists: we did the legal reading so they just avoid the words.

## The workflow (what Giovanna's team runs)

1. **Onboard** — send guidelines → get written acknowledgment → countersigned contract with claims annex.
2. **Equip** — share the current approved claims list; update creators when it changes.
3. **Post** — creator publishes with disclosure.
4. **Spot-check** — monthly review of tagged content against the checklist.
5. **Fix** — non-compliant post → correction request → confirm within 48h → log it.
6. **Escalate** — repeat issues → pause partnership → document. Questions about a borderline claim → route to legal before it posts.

## Why each piece matters (if asked)

- *Why monitoring?* The FTC's revised Endorsement Guides say brands are liable for endorser statements and expect a real program: guidance, training, monitoring, and consequences. Paper guidelines without enforcement are worse than nothing in an investigation.
- *Why exact claim language?* NAD killed Coterie's "up to 4x absorbency vs. leading brands" (P&G challenge, 2025) because the testing didn't match the words. Paraphrasing a substantiated claim makes it an unsubstantiated one.
- *Why the rash-language obsession?* "Prevents/treats diaper rash" is an FDA drug claim (OTC skin-protectant monograph). "Helps keep skin dry" is a diaper claim. One word separates them.
- *Why no eco words?* gDiapers settled FTC charges over "biodegradable"/"flushable" diaper claims. We don't currently have substantiation for any general environmental-benefit claim.
