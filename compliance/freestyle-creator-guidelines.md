# Freestyle Creator & Influencer Guidelines

**For anyone posting about Freestyle diapers or wipes as part of a paid, gifted, affiliate, or ambassador relationship.**

Thanks for partnering with Freestyle! We want your content to sound like *you* — but because you're posting about a baby product, the FTC treats your posts as Freestyle advertising. These guidelines keep both of us protected. They apply to every platform: Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Facebook, X, Pinterest, blogs, podcasts, and livestreams.

**The two rules that matter most:**
1. **Always disclose the relationship** — clearly, at the start, on every post.
2. **Stick to the approved claims** — share your real experience, but don't invent product facts, comparisons, or health promises.

---

## 1. Disclosure: when and how

### When you must disclose
If Freestyle gave you **anything** — payment, free or discounted product, an affiliate commission, a contest entry, or an ongoing ambassador relationship — you have a "material connection" and must disclose it on **every single post**, even if:
- You weren't told what to say
- You'd genuinely recommend the product anyway
- You disclosed in an earlier post (each post stands alone)
- The post is "just" a Story, comment, reply, or livestream

### What counts as a good disclosure
Use plain language people instantly understand:
- ✅ **#ad** or **#FreestylePartner** (paired with #ad)
- ✅ "Sponsored by Freestyle"
- ✅ "Freestyle sent me these diapers to try"
- ✅ "This is a paid partnership with Freestyle"
- ✅ "I earn a commission if you use my link"

These are **NOT** enough on their own:
- ❌ #sp, #spon, #collab, #ambassador, #partner (too vague)
- ❌ "Thanks Freestyle!" with no mention of payment/free product
- ❌ Tagging @freestyle or using a discount code as the only signal
- ❌ Disclosure buried below "…more," in a wall of hashtags, in the comments, or only on your profile/bio
- ❌ Relying **only** on the platform's built-in "Paid partnership" label — use it, but add your own disclosure too

### Where it goes, by platform
- **Instagram/Facebook feed:** at the *beginning* of the caption, before the "…more" cutoff
- **TikTok/Reels/Shorts:** say it out loud in the first few seconds **and** put it in the on-screen text or caption — many viewers watch muted, others never read captions
- **YouTube:** verbal disclosure early in the video **and** in the description
- **Stories/Snapchat:** text overlay directly on the image/video, large enough to read, on every frame that features the product
- **Livestreams:** repeat the disclosure periodically — viewers drop in mid-stream
- **Podcasts/audio:** spoken disclosure before or during the mention
- **X/Twitter, Threads:** disclosure inside the post itself (e.g., start with "Ad:")

### No "door openers"
Don't post something that looks like an impartial question or independent review ("Which diaper is actually best? 🧐 link in bio") that leads to sponsored content. The disclosure has to be at the **first** point of contact, not just at the destination. (A diaper brand was cited by the National Advertising Division in 2025 for exactly this.)

## 2. Talking about the product: say this, not that

Share your honest, first-hand experience — that's why we work with you. What you **can't** do is state product facts, test results, or health claims we haven't substantiated. Use the approved language below; when in doubt, describe what *you* saw with *your* baby instead of making a general claim.

### Absorbency & leaks
- ✅ "These are seriously absorbent — we've had way fewer blowouts."
- ✅ "Kept my baby dry overnight." / "We made it through a 12-hour night, no leaks." *(your real experience)*
- ✅ Approved brand claim, quoted exactly as we provide it (e.g., the BambooTek™ absorbency comparison) — use the exact wording and any required disclosure we give you; don't round up, drop the qualifier, or paraphrase.
- ❌ "The most absorbent diaper on the market" / "more absorbent than any other diaper"
- ❌ Making up or rounding numbers ("like 10x more absorbent than Pampers")
- ❌ "Guaranteed no leaks / never leaks" — say what happened for you, not a promise for every baby
- ❌ Naming competitor brands in comparisons unless we've given you approved, substantiated language

### Materials & "clean" positioning
- ✅ "The core is made from bamboo." / "Plant-based materials where it counts."
- ✅ "Free of fragrances and phthalates" *(only the specific "free-of" statements on current packaging/brand materials)*
- ✅ "Designed for sensitive skin — my baby's skin has been so happy."
- ❌ "100% natural" / "all-natural" / "chemical-free" — everything is made of chemicals; regulators treat these as deceptive
- ❌ "Non-toxic" as your own blanket characterization beyond approved brand language, and never "other diapers are toxic / full of chemicals" — disparaging competitors with safety scare claims is a fast track to legal trouble
- ❌ "Organic" (unless it appears in current approved brand materials for a specific component, quoted exactly)

### Skin health & diaper rash — the biggest tripwire
Claiming a product **prevents, treats, or cures** diaper rash, eczema, or any skin condition is a *drug claim* regulated by the FDA. Diapers aren't approved drugs, so:
- ✅ "Helps keep baby's skin dry." / "We've had way less redness and irritation since switching." *(personal observation)*
- ✅ "Gentle on my baby's sensitive skin."
- ❌ "Prevents diaper rash" / "cures diaper rash" / "cleared up my baby's eczema" / "healed her rash in two days"
- ❌ "Dermatologist recommended" / "pediatrician approved" (unless we supply that claim with substantiation)
- ❌ Any "safer than," "hypoallergenic," or allergy-related promise in your own words — if it's on our packaging, quote it exactly; don't extend it

Even a *true personal story* phrased as "this cured my baby's rash" can create liability, because viewers hear it as a promise about their baby. Frame it as your experience with dryness and comfort, not a medical outcome.

### Environmental claims
"Green" claims are tightly policed under the FTC's Green Guides, and a diaper brand (gDiapers) has already been through an FTC enforcement action here.
- ✅ "The core is bamboo, a fast-growing plant." / "Less plastic than conventional diapers" *(only if provided as an approved claim)*
- ❌ "Biodegradable," "compostable," "eco-friendly," "sustainable," "zero waste," "good for the planet" — never use these unless we hand you exact approved language, which we currently do not
- ❌ "Flushable" (never)

### Safety & absolutes
- ❌ "The safest diaper you can buy" / "the only diaper I'd trust"— superlatives about safety are unsubstantiable
- ❌ "No harmful ingredients whatsoever"
- ❌ Anything implying other brands hurt babies

## 3. Honesty basics (the non-negotiables)

- **Actually use the product.** Only post about Freestyle if you've genuinely used it with your child, and keep posting only while you're a genuine user.
- **Say what you believe.** Never state opinions you don't hold or experiences you didn't have. If you didn't love something, you're not obligated to post — but don't fake enthusiasm.
- **Don't misrepresent yourself.** Don't present sponsored content as an independent review, and don't pose as "just a mom who found this" when you're a paid partner.
- **Reviews:** Don't post star ratings/reviews on retail sites (Amazon, Target, etc.) in exchange for payment or free product unless the platform allows it AND the review discloses it. We will never ask you for a positive review — under the FTC's 2024 Reviews Rule, incentives conditioned on positive sentiment are illegal for us to offer.
- **Kids' privacy & platforms:** follow each platform's branded-content and children's-content policies; don't direct advertising at children (our audience is parents).
- **AI content:** if you use AI-generated imagery of "your baby" or fabricated scenarios presented as real, that's a misrepresentation. Real product, real use.

---

## 4. Quick pre-post checklist

1. ☐ Disclosure in plain words (**#ad** or equivalent) at the **top** of the caption
2. ☐ Spoken disclosure in the first few seconds (video) + on-screen text
3. ☐ Platform "Paid partnership" tool turned on (in addition to, not instead of)
4. ☐ Only approved claims, quoted exactly — everything else is your own first-hand experience, framed as *your* experience
5. ☐ No rash/health "prevents, treats, cures" language; no "chemical-free," "biodegradable," "hypoallergenic," "safest," "guaranteed"
6. ☐ No competitor call-outs or made-up statistics
7. ☐ You actually use the product and mean what you say

**Questions or a claim you're not sure about?** Send us the draft — we'd always rather review a caption than run a correction. Contact your Freestyle partnerships manager.

---

*These guidelines summarize FTC requirements (FTC Act §5; Endorsement Guides, 16 CFR Part 255, revised 2023; Consumer Reviews & Testimonials Rule, 16 CFR Part 465; Green Guides, 16 CFR Part 260) and related FDA rules. They're not legal advice to you personally, but following them keeps everyone on the right side of the regulators — and keeps the partnership fun.*
